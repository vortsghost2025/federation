How to Play the Control Plane

You are governing the Federation. Each turn presents a constitutional constraint — a crisis, policy decision, or external pressure — and you must choose how to respond.

  • Read the Current Constraint at the top center. It describes the situation, the domain, what rights are at stake, and the risk level.
  • Choose an action by clicking one of the large buttons below the constraint. Each choice has different consequences.
  • Review the outcome — after each choice, an overlay shows what changed: metric deltas, explainability, and any subsystem effects (rival activity, political developments, history arc movement).
  • Monitor your Governance Vectors in the left column: Stability, Public Trust, Council Support, Constitutional Integrity, Rights Protection, Emergency Power. These determine whether your Federation survives.
  • Watch for the No Gate. Some choices can be refused by the constitutional lattice if emergency power is too high or provenance is weak. A "NO GATE" label means the system blocked your action.
  • Survive 100 turns with Stability above 30, Trust above 20, Constitutional Integrity above 20, and Rights above 20 to achieve victory.
  • Rival Federations act each turn — hostile rivals damage you, friendly rivals offer bonuses. The Consciousness Sheet tracks the Federation's psychological state.

The left column shows governance state, the center is your decision space, and the right column is your decision ledger and institutional thesis.

Core Thesis

Governance is state transition discipline. Every choice mutates constraints. The question is whether the mutation preserves constitutional viability.

No Gate

The lattice can say no. A tempting action can be rejected when provenance is weak, lane ownership is unclear, or emergency power exceeds constitutional bounds.
